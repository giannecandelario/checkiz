# Checkiz

**For small online sellers who get paid by personal e-wallet: know which orders were really paid, and catch reused or mismatched receipts, without checking the app one screenshot at a time.**

Checkiz is a triage tool for Filipino online sellers who accept GCash/Maya-style transfers. Upload or photograph a receipt and get a plain verdict. Where possible it confirms the receipt against payment alerts the seller already receives, and it can settle an order from the alert alone, with no screenshot.

Built for CSSPRINT 2026 (UST CICS Computer Science Society), problem statement: *a practical digital solution to a specific problem faced by Filipino MSMEs.*

> **Status: prototype.** Verdict logic, alert parsing and settlement are covered by 197 automated tests. The phone alert feed and wallet behaviour have **not** been tested on a real device yet - [`docs/DEVICE_TEST.md`](docs/DEVICE_TEST.md) is the protocol for that, and no table in it has been filled. Every feature row below is reconciled against the code in [`docs/STATUS.md`](docs/STATUS.md).

## Who it is for

**One user: the small online seller.** Someone who sells through Facebook, TikTok or Viber (often by live selling), takes personal-wallet transfers, ships after payment, and confirms payments by hand with no payment gateway.

**One job:** for each order, know whether it was really paid, and spot reused or mismatched receipts without opening the wallet app for every screenshot.

A typical routine this is built for (a hypothetical, **not** from an interview): the seller goes live a few nights a week, collects transfers, checks screenshots against the app late at night, and ships the next day.

**Buyers are not the target user.** They are the other side of the transaction. Their experience still matters, because order binding only works if paying the exact amount is easy - which is why a simple buyer pay page is planned (not built yet; see [Features](#features)).

Not the focus for now (see [Future directions](#future-directions)): stores with staff, and freelancers.

## The problem

Sellers take orders from comments during a live, then collect and confirm payments afterwards, often by chat and a spreadsheet. The official advice from banks and wallets is that a screenshot is not proof: check the transaction history in the app. At volume that is slow, and a fake can get through when the seller is tired or rushed. A fake receipt can carry a plausible reference number and amount. Wallets have also warned about receipts generated by AI apps.

I did not find seller interviews or measured fraud rates for sellers, so this describes the workflow and official guidance, **not** how often fakes succeed. Validating this with real sellers is on the roadmap.

## What it tells you

| Verdict | Meaning | How reliable |
|---|---|---|
| **Verified** | The receipt's reference and amount match a payment alert on the seller's own ledger | Strongest signal. Only as trustworthy as the alert feed |
| **Reused** | This reference number, or this exact image file, was already checked for another order | Reliable for repeats of a reference number or of the identical file. A copy re-compressed by a messenger app has different bytes and is caught only by the reference |
| **Doesn't match** | The amount differs from the alert, or from the amount issued for this order | Reliable when a ledger entry exists |
| **Likely edited** | Image signals suggest tampering | **Weak. See below** |
| **Can't confirm yet** | Nothing contradicts the receipt and nothing confirms it either: no matching payment has arrived, or the image could not be read | Honest default. Wait for the alert, check the wallet app, or retake the photo |

The API returns both a machine status (`VERIFIED`, `DUPLICATE`, `SUSPECTED_FAKE`, `PENDING_MATCH`) and the plain `label` above. The codes did not change; the label is derived from the code plus which check produced it, because `SUSPECTED_FAKE` covers three different things a seller should do next. A receipt that cannot be read is labelled "Can't confirm yet", not "Likely edited" - nothing was contradicted. The dashboard, overlay, review queue and evidence view show the labels. Mapping: [`backend/app/labels.py`](backend/app/labels.py), tests in `backend/tests/test_labels.py`.

### Signals, not proof

Four checks ask what a receipt says about itself, with no payment alert involved ([`backend/app/consistency.py`](backend/app/consistency.py)):

| Check | Example of what it reports |
|---|---|
| Reference format | "No 13-digit reference found; the closest number read was 100284913049, 12 digits" |
| Amount format and range | "Amount 60000.00 is outside the 1.00-50000.00 range this shop is set for" |
| Printed date and time | "Receipt shows 2026-10-01 09:00, about 534 hours old - this shop treats 48 hours as current"; a stamp later than this clock |
| Layout against the template | "Reference 1002849130490 was read from a line with no reference label beside it" |

**None of them can change a verdict.** A genuine receipt fails these for ordinary reasons: the photo came in at an odd angle, a wallet words its receipt differently, the phone's clock and the server's do not agree, or the shop has not set its own amount range yet. So a signal is a line to read next to the verdict, never a "fake". The hard rules are unchanged: no reference to match on, a reference already claimed, an amount that contradicts the ledger or the order's coded QR.

They appear as `signals` in the verdict response, in the review queue, in the evidence timeline, and as a count on the OBS overlay. The ranges, the accepted age of a receipt, the date spellings and the label words are all environment variables (below) because they belong to the shop and the wallet, not to the code. A time printed without a date is deliberately not checked: `20:05` could be ten minutes ago or last night, and a guess there would produce signals a seller stops reading.

### When the photo, not the receipt, is the problem

Photographing a screen is where OCR is weakest: glare, an angle, a small font. Tesseract scores every word it reads, and Checkiz averages those scores into `confidence` (0-100) on the verdict. Under `CHECKIZ_OCR_CONFIDENCE_MIN` (60) the dashboard adds one line - *"Retake for a clearer photo: this one read at 34/100. Fill the frame with the receipt, hold the screen square to the camera, and tilt it away from glare."* (the 34 is read by a stub in the tests and the demo; no photo of a real receipt was scored that low here) - and **nothing else changes**. The verdict, the signals and the status codes are computed exactly as they would be without it, because a blurred photo of a genuine payment is still a genuine payment.

The number describes one attempt at one photo, so it is not stored - the advice is only useful while the cashier is still holding the phone. Both shipped synthetic samples read at about 94 (table below), and the edited one scored a fraction *higher* than the real one: this measures legibility, never tampering. **No phone photo of a real wallet receipt has been measured**, so 60 is a starting point, not a calibrated threshold.

**What Checkiz cannot do:** prove a receipt is fake from the image alone. A clean, AI-generated receipt can look perfect. Image analysis is a secondary signal. **Confirm large payments in the wallet app.**

### Measured so far (honest)

On the shipped *synthetic* sample pair (not real receipts), at default thresholds (ELA 8.0, sharpness 6.0), run through real Tesseract in the Docker image:

| Sample | ELA | Sharpness | Read score | Verdict | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `real.png` | 5.78 | 4.08 | 94.5 | VERIFIED | 582 ms |
| `edited.png` | 5.82 | 4.08 | 94.6 | SUSPECTED FAKE | 693 ms |

**The forensic check did not separate the two.** The edited receipt was caught only because its amount (9250.00) did not match the ledger alert (1250.00). Thresholds need calibration on genuine receipts before any claim about edit detection. [`docs/CALIBRATION.md`](docs/CALIBRATION.md) is the guide and `backend/calibrate.py` is the script.

The read-score column is today's run (Tesseract 5.5.0 in the same image, the default confidence floor of 60): neither photo is anywhere near it, and the edited copy scored marginally higher, so confidence says nothing about tampering. That run also read the printed date and time out of real OCR rather than a stub - `Oct 22, 2026` and `10:14 AM` on both samples - though the pages themselves are still the synthetic renders, not a wallet's receipt.

The same pair measured through `calibrate.py` itself, which is the shape a real run pastes into (Tesseract 5.5.0, thresholds ELA > 8.0 / sharpness > 6.0, n = 1 and 1, run date 2026-10-22 as the container keeps UTC):

| Sample | Group | ELA | Sharpness | Read score | Flagged at these thresholds | Time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `real.png` | real | 5.78 | 4.08 | 94.5 | not flagged | 610 ms |
| `edited.png` | edited | 5.82 | 4.08 | 94.6 | not flagged | 550 ms |

The "not flagged" column is the bold sentence above, in the script's own words: at the shipped thresholds neither image is flagged, so the edited copy is invisible to the forensics. `--at 5.8 4.09` on these two files does flag the edited one, and the script refuses to turn that into a recommended threshold, because n = 1 per group is not a measurement.

## What makes it different: order binding

The obvious question is "is this receipt real?". Checkiz also asks "was this payment made for *this order*?"

1. The seller generates a QR Ph for each order with a fixed amount and a free centavo code (for example PHP 150.37), so every open order has a different amount.
2. When the matching alert arrives, **the order settles itself**, with no screenshot and no need to open the wallet app.
3. If a buyer also sends a receipt, its amount must equal the amount issued for that order. A genuine receipt from another order cannot be reused, and one payment settles only one order.

Limits: it only covers orders made through Checkiz's QR page. The wallet may ignore the reference label (the centavo code is the fallback). An unrelated payment with exactly the same amount as an open order, inside the time window (`CHECKIZ_ORDER_TTL_HOURS`, default 24), would match it.

## How it works

1. **Alert feed:** the seller's own payment alerts reach the ledger through (a) wallet-app notifications, (b) SMS, (c) pasted text, or (d) a screenshot of the wallet's own transaction list when none of the first three arrives. Each entry records its source, and sources have different trust (see below).
2. **Receipt check:** the receipt is uploaded or photographed. OCR extracts the reference number, the amount, and the date and time printed on the receipt. The file's hash is compared with earlier checks, then reference format and replay, then the ledger. Alongside the verdict, the receipt is also read against itself (see **Signals, not proof** below), and the photo is scored for legibility (see **When the photo, not the receipt, is the problem**).
3. **Settlement:** a ledger match (or an alert that matches an open coded order) settles the order. The order is locked to that payment.
4. **Review queue:** anything unresolved, suspicious, duplicate, or needing an in-app confirmation is listed for the seller. Receipt images are not stored, only extracted fields and the verdict.

### Alert trust levels

| Source | Endpoint | Trust | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wallet-app notification | `POST /ledger/notification` | Highest | Android sets the package name, so a texter cannot fake it. Package allow-list: `CHECKIZ_APP_PACKAGES` |
| SMS | `POST /ledger/sms` | Medium | Sender IDs can be spoofed. Large SMS-only payments return `needs_app_check` |
| Pasted text | `POST /ledger/paste` | Medium | Fallback, for example text copied from the in-app inbox |
| Your own transactions screen, screenshotted | `POST /ledger/history-screenshot` | Lowest | Reads the list row by row (reference, amount, time) when nothing arrives at all. Unsigned, OCR'd off an image, and a reference can attach to the wrong amount on a crooked list, so it is never treated as the wallet confirming anything |

GCash moved send-money and other confirmations from SMS to its app inbox starting in 2022, so **SMS may not arrive at all**. Check what your own phone receives - [`docs/DEVICE_TEST.md`](docs/DEVICE_TEST.md) is the protocol for that, with the tables to fill in. Whether the wallet app posts a push notification for each incoming payment is **still unconfirmed: nobody has run that protocol yet**, and every table in it is empty on purpose.

The tool is not tied to one wallet: reconciliation keys on reference and amount, trusted apps and reference length are configurable (`CHECKIZ_APP_PACKAGES`, `CHECKIZ_REF_LENGTH`), and order binding uses QR Ph, which is payable from bank and e-money apps. Parsers currently require a `PHP`/`₱` amount with two decimals, an English money-in word (`received`, `credited`, `money in`, `you got`) and a `Ref` label; without a reference label an entry gets a synthetic key and loses replay detection.

## How it compares

Checkiz is a low-friction first check. It is not better overall than the options below and does not replace them.

| Option | Good at | Where Checkiz differs |
|---|---|---|
| Checking the wallet app by hand (the advice from GCash and banks) | Most reliable check there is | Slow at volume. Checkiz automates the same comparison from the alert feed and still asks for in-app confirmation of large SMS-only payments |
| Licensed gateways and GCash for Business (for example [HitPay](https://hitpayapp.com/ph/), [PayMongo](https://www.paymongo.com/blog/how-to-accept-gcash-payments)) | Confirmation comes from the payment system itself, which Checkiz cannot match. Payment links are built for social sellers | Need merchant verification (requirements vary by provider), and buyers pay through the link or QR. Checkiz works with ordinary personal-wallet transfers with no onboarding |
| Wallet soundbox/payment devices (for example GCash SoundPay) | Audio confirmation that an amount arrived | A static QR shows an amount landed, not which order. I could not confirm whether newer models show dynamic QR (the vendor pages block automated access) |
| Live-selling and order tools ([BigSeller](https://www.bigseller.com/en_US/index.htm), [EasyStore](https://www.easystore.co/en-ph), [Prosperna](https://www.prosperna.com/)) | Order capture, checkout links, shipping, marketplace reconciliation | Their own documentation, opened 2026-10-23, describes manual confirmation of personal-wallet payments: Prosperna states it [does not auto-detect payments](https://help.prosperna.com/payments/how-to-set-up-direct-transfer-payments), EasyStore's manual path says to [verify and mark the order paid yourself](https://www.easystore.co/en-ph/payments), and BigSeller's ["Payment Reconciliation"](https://help.bigseller.com/en_US/detailPage/what-is-autocheck-payment-certificate.9328) covers marketplace payouts. Not exhaustively verified. Checkiz focuses on tying a payment to an order and could sit beside them |
| Receipt/slip image verifiers | Exist as a category - [BigSeller's Slip2Go verifies Thai bank transfer certificates](https://help.bigseller.com/en_US/detailPage/what-is-autocheck-payment-certificate.9328) | **Checkiz is not the first receipt checker.** The claim is order binding on personal wallets plus reuse detection |

## Optional AI-assisted reading (planned, off by default)

Phone photos of screens are where plain OCR is weakest. An optional vision model may read the reference, amount and time more accurately - standard OCR now extracts a printed date and time as well, but only from receipts whose wording it recognises, so reading them from a glare-covered photo is still something the model would have to prove. It is **extraction only**: it never decides whether a receipt is real. If it disagrees with standard OCR, the result is "Can't confirm yet". It requires explicit consent, is off by default, and falls back automatically to standard OCR when unavailable. It will be enabled as an option only if a measured comparison on real and edited receipts (including phone photos) shows an improvement; the README will report that result with the number of receipts, including if it shows none. Provider and data terms read before this was written: [`docs/AI_PRIVACY.md`](docs/AI_PRIVACY.md).

## Privacy and responsible use

- Reading payment notifications or SMS involves personal and financial data. Philippine privacy rules call for consent that is freely given, specific and informed, with the purpose explained. Show a plain consent screen, collect only what matching needs, process locally where possible, and let sellers delete their data. This is not legal advice.
- Google Play restricts the SMS permission group to default handlers and a few exceptions, and I did not verify its rules for notification access. The prototype is sideloaded, not distributed through Play. See https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9047303
- Set `CHECKIZ_API_KEY`. Without it, anyone who can reach the server can post fake alerts. The server warns at startup (`backend/app/main.py:53`). The dashboard and QR page ask for it once and keep it in `sessionStorage`, which the browser drops when the tab closes - it is deliberately not saved to disk, because a till machine is often shared.
- Values that originate in alert text (sender names, reference strings) are neutralised before they reach the packing CSV, so a spreadsheet cannot execute a formula hidden in an SMS.

## Limitations

- **The ledger is only as good as the alert feed.** SMS may not arrive, SMS sender IDs can be spoofed, and the notification path is unproven on a real phone.
- **Edit detection is weak and uncalibrated.** It may miss AI-generated receipts entirely. Rely on ledger matching.
- Auto-settlement only covers orders made through Checkiz's QR, and amount-only matching can collide with an unrelated payment of the same amount.
- The problem description comes from bank, wallet, news and vendor sources, not seller interviews. How often fakes succeed is unmeasured.
- Receipt layouts and alert wording change when wallets update, so parsers need maintenance.
- Checkiz is a triage layer, not proof of funds. It does not replace confirming in the wallet app or using a licensed gateway, and it never holds money.

## Getting started

```bash
# 1. Install Tesseract (Ubuntu: sudo apt install tesseract-ocr | macOS: brew install tesseract | Windows: UB-Mannheim build, add to PATH)

# 2. Backend - it serves the dashboard too, so there is only one thing to run
cd backend
python -m venv .venv && source .venv/bin/activate   # Windows: .venv\Scripts\activate
pip install -r requirements.txt
python seed.py            # seeds the mock ledger
python make_samples.py    # writes synthetic samples/real.png and samples/edited.png
uvicorn app.main:app --reload
```

Open **http://localhost:8000/**. The pages and the API share that one origin, so no page has to be told where the other half lives.

If step 1 was skipped, the server says so twice instead of failing silently: `/health` answers `{"ocr": null}`, the dashboard opens with "This server cannot read any receipt yet", and an upload answers **503** naming Tesseract rather than a 500 stack trace. No verdict is written for it, because nothing was read.

- **From a phone on the same network:** run `uvicorn app.main:app --host 0.0.0.0 --port 8000` and open `http://<lan-ip>:8000/`. The in-page camera only works over https or localhost, so on a plain LAN address the photo buttons fall back to the phone's own camera - the reason is shown on screen when that happens.
- **If another project is already on 8000:** start Checkiz on a free port and open that. A page pointed at something that is not Checkiz now says what answered it instead of reporting "could not reach the server".
- **Two Checkiz instances at once** (a demo over someone's data, a device test): give the second one its own file with `CHECKIZ_DB_PATH=/tmp/demo.db`.

Tests: `cd backend && pytest` (OCR is stubbed, Tesseract not needed). Docker: `docker build -t checkiz . && docker run -p 8000:8000 checkiz`.

A scripted demo on a throwaway database (`backend/demo.py`) is planned and **not in the repo yet**; until it exists, the way to see the logic without a phone is to upload the synthetic samples above. It would use the **synthetic** samples, so it shows the logic, not accuracy on real receipts.

Environment variables: `CHECKIZ_API_KEY`, `CHECKIZ_ORDER_TTL_HOURS` (24), `CHECKIZ_CONFIRM_ABOVE` (2000), `CHECKIZ_APP_PACKAGES`, `CHECKIZ_REF_LENGTH` (13), `CHECKIZ_ELA_THRESHOLD`, `CHECKIZ_SHARPNESS_THRESHOLD`, `CHECKIZ_MAX_UPLOAD_MB` (8), `CHECKIZ_OCR_CONFIDENCE_MIN` (60, the floor under which a photo is called retake-worthy), `CHECKIZ_DB_PATH` (`backend/checkiz.db`, so a second instance can be given its own file), `CHECKIZ_FRONTEND` (the folder beside this backend, if the pages live elsewhere). For the consistency signals: `CHECKIZ_AMOUNT_MIN` (1.00), `CHECKIZ_AMOUNT_MAX` (50000.00), `CHECKIZ_RECEIPT_MAX_AGE_HOURS` (48), `CHECKIZ_TZ_OFFSET_HOURS` (8), `CHECKIZ_DATE_FORMATS`, `CHECKIZ_AMOUNT_LABELS`, `CHECKIZ_REF_LABELS`, `CHECKIZ_LAYOUT_LABEL_WINDOW` (1). The two label lists are what make the layout check follow a wallet's wording instead of assuming one.

### Demo scenarios

| Scenario | Input | Expected |
|---|---|---|
| Screenshot-free payment | Buyer pays the exact coded amount of an order | Auto-settled, no receipt needed |
| Edited amount | Receipt with a changed amount | Doesn't match: receipt amount differs from the ledger alert |
| Real receipt for another order | Genuine receipt submitted for a different coded order | Rejected: amount belongs to a different order |
| Genuine receipt | Matches a ledger alert | Verified |
| Reused receipt | Same reference again | Reused |
| Receipt that contradicts itself | Amount outside the shop's set range, or a printed time later than this clock | Verdict unchanged, the reason listed under `signals` |
| Photo that reads poorly | The reader's own mean score falls under the floor (the test reads it at 34/100) | Verdict unchanged, one extra line: retake it |
| Alerts never arrived | Screenshot of the seller's own transaction list uploaded | Its incoming rows enter the ledger as `history`; money-out rows are refused with a reason; uploading the same screen again adds nothing |

Each row above is asserted by a test in `backend/tests/`. Demo order note: upload `edited.png` before `real.png`, because the edited copy shares the real receipt's reference number.

## Future directions

Not built, not validated, and deliberately out of scope for now:
- **Stores with staff:** employees photograph customers' receipts; the owner wants to catch fake receipts and mismatches between logged sales and received payments. It would need staff PINs, a record of who submitted each check, and end-of-day reconciliation in both directions. Keep the alert feed on the owner's device, and treat flags as prompts for a human to look, not proof against an employee. This also competes with counter devices such as wallet soundboxes.
- **Verified sales record:** an export of settled orders for bookkeeping and possibly, later, access to credit. Whether lenders would accept it is unknown.
- **Messenger / Viber intake.**
- **Freelancers:** not researched; their payments are typically fewer and invoice-based, which is a different problem.
